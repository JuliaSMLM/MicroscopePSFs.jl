Multi-channel PSFs

PlanePSF shifts an emitter's axial coordinate, AnalyzerPSF selects a vector field component, and FourPiPSF interferes two opposed objectives. Each is a complete 3D AbstractPSF that can be evaluated, integrated, tabulated as a SplinePSF, and saved. MultiChannelPSF holds complete channel PSFs and their registration and photon fractions. It deliberately does not subtype AbstractPSF, so a one-channel fitter must handle channels explicitly.

Coordinates and the forward law

The one definition of the container is

expected image i = N * mc.transmission[i] *
    integrate_pixels(mc.channels[i], camera_i, unit-photon emitter at channel_position(mc, i, x, y, z))

N is the emitter's total photons. When transmissions sum below 1, photons mean photons before those losses. Registration matrices have size 2×3 and map absolute camera coordinates in microns, as used by SMLMData pixel edges: [xᵢ, yᵢ] = A * [x, y] + t. They do not map pixels or crop-local coordinates; keeping the original pixel edges makes the same registration valid after crops. The axial law is zᵢ = z + mc.z_offset[i]. The reference channel must have identity registration and zero axial offset. Missing transforms, fractions, and axial offsets default to identity, equal splitting, and zeros.

Each channel must integrate to one alone. For 4Pi phase channels this means unit energy on average over the fringe, rather than at every emitter z. Transmission is a nonnegative fraction of one total photon budget, with sum at most one. The constructor cannot check the channel normalization. In particular, raw H/V AnalyzerPSFs carry the polarization split themselves and are refused, including through PlanePSF and either arm of FourPiPSF. Tabulate these channels into a SplinePSF scaled to unit integral and put the split in transmission.

Dual focal plane

using MicroscopePSFs

p = ScalarPSF(1.2, 0.68, 1.33)
mc = MultiChannelPSF(; channels=[p, PlanePSF(p; dz=0.2)],
                     transmission=[0.5, 0.5])
camera = IdealCamera(collect(0.0:0.1:2.0), collect(0.0:0.1:2.0))
N = 1000.0
images = [mc.transmission[i] * integrate_pixels(mc.channels[i], camera,
    Emitter3D(channel_position(mc, i, 1.0, 1.0, 0.1)..., N)) for i in 1:2]

PlanePSF(p; dz) evaluates every method at z + dz. This is emitter-side high-NA defocus. It is not the same phase as translating a camera in low-NA image space; a learned pupil absorbs that difference. Use either a plane wrapper or a container z_offset for a given shift to avoid adding it twice. Learned containers carry axial offsets in z_offset; PlanePSF is for building model channels. A learned channel's offset is never applied twice. A plane can wrap a 3D intensity spline, but splines cannot supply coherent fields to an analyzer or an interferometer.

H/V polarization

An analyzer passes Ex for component=1 (H) or Ey for component=2 (V), zeroing the rejected component for every dipole. It does not renormalize, so h(x,y,z) + v(x,y,z) ≈ p(x,y,z). Rotating dipoles remain mutually incoherent. dipole_fields preserves that distinction, returning one component vector per dipole. amplitude is meaningful only for a single dipole.

This example tabulates a focal slice and normalizes each channel alone. Increase the field of view for more accurate collected photon fractions. For a 3D calibration, normalize each channel using its calibrated energy convention, preserving the relative intensities of its axial slices.

using MicroscopePSFs

p = VectorPSF(1.2, 0.68; n_medium=1.33, n_coverslip=1.33,
              n_immersion=1.33, grid_size=64)
h, v = AnalyzerPSF(p; component=1), AnalyzerPSF(p; component=2)
r = -1.0:0.05:1.0
area = step(r)^2
grids = [[a(x, y, 0.0) for y in r, x in r] for a in (h, v)]
energies = sum.(grids) .* area
channels = [SplinePSF(g ./ e, r, r) for (g, e) in zip(grids, energies)]
mc = MultiChannelPSF(; channels, transmission=energies ./ sum(energies))

4Pi interference

using MicroscopePSFs

arm = ScalarPSF(1.2, 0.68, 1.33)
p₀ = FourPiPSF(arm, arm; phase=0.0)
pπ = FourPiPSF(arm, arm; phase=π)
mc = MultiChannelPSF(; channels=[p₀, pπ], transmission=[0.5, 0.5])
intensity = mc.channels[1](0.1, 0.0, 0.2)

The objectives share a focal plane; the top sees z and the bottom sees -z. The two images overlap with the same orientation. One arm therefore has one more mirror than the other, and both arms' camera x and y components lie in a common frame. For dipole d,

\[E_d = \frac{E_{t,d}(x,y,z) + e^{i\phi}s_d E_{b,d}(x,y,-z)}{\sqrt{2}}, \qquad p = \sum_d \lVert E_d\rVert^2.\]

phase is the cavity phase. Polarization-dependent cavity phases use one AnalyzerPSF(FourPiPSF(top, bottom; phase=φ); component=c) per channel, followed by the same channel normalization convention used for H/V. Vector arms require n_medium ≥ NA: otherwise evanescent fields grow without bound at negative emitter z, which one of the arms always sees.

The factor 1/√2 makes unit-energy arms give a unit-energy channel averaged over the fringe. A 4Pi port's transmission is its fringe-average share, 0.5 for each of two complementary ports. Fitted photons are the fringe-average total. A single 4Pi channel at transmission 1 predicts up to twice that total at the fringe peak. This photon convention also applies to learned 4Pi channels. Complementary phase channels obey p(φ) + p(φ + π) ≈ top(x,y,z) + bottom(x,y,-z) when the mirror signs are all ones or for a rotating dipole.

Why the axial dipole changes sign

A dipole radiates the transverse far field p - dot(p, k̂) * k̂. A lens rotates this field onto its optical axis. Reflection of the opposed geometry changes pz to -pz while leaving px and py unchanged. After the arm mirrors align the camera components, the x and y dipoles are even and the axial dipole is odd. A rotating dipole thus needs signs (1, 1, -1) in x, y, z dipole order. Its three intensities add incoherently, but each dipole's two arms interfere.

For a fixed dipole, build the bottom arm with DipoleVector(px, py, -pz); the mirror is already in its orientation, so its remaining field sign is +1. Every DipoleVector constructor normalizes nonzero inputs. The 4Pi constructor compares the mirrored directions within the coarser arm's precision.

Emitter z and dipole z share one frame, with +z pointing into the sample, away from the objective. A code dipole DipoleVector(1.0, 0, 1.0) matches lab orientation [1, 0, 1]/√2. The opposed-objective mirror rule applies:

using MicroscopePSFs

optics = (; n_medium=1.33, n_coverslip=1.33, n_immersion=1.33, grid_size=64)
top = VectorPSF(1.2, 0.68, DipoleVector(1.0, 0, 1.0); optics...)
bottom = VectorPSF(1.2, 0.68, DipoleVector(1.0, 0, -1.0); optics...)
p = FourPiPSF(top, bottom; phase=0.3)
fields = dipole_fields(p, 0.1, 0.0, 0.2)
